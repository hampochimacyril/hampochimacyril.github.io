About

I study how hot and humid U.S. homes get indoors, who is most exposed, and what that means for health — then I turn those methods into deployed, inspectable software rather than leaving them in a notebook.

My doctoral research bridges architectural engineering and environmental public health: EnergyPlus / ResStock-style building-stock simulation, hybrid physics-and-ML modeling, and rigorous uncertainty analysis — built into health-linkage-ready exposure metrics. It is funded by the NIH (1R01HL164726-01).

Before Drexel I completed a research master's in mechanical engineering, publishing across district cooling, life-cycle assessment, and energy systems. The throughline is constant: take a hard technical problem and ship something people can actually use.
