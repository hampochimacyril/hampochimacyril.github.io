About

Where building science meets public health — and ships as code.

I study how hot and humid U.S. homes get indoors, who is most exposed, and what that means for health. My toolkit spans EnergyPlus / ResStock-style building-stock simulation, hybrid physics-based and machine-learning modeling, and rigorous uncertainty analysis. What sets my work apart is that it doesn't end in a notebook — I turn research-grade methods into deployed, inspectable products. My doctoral research is funded by the NIH (1R01HL164726-01) and bridges architectural engineering and environmental public health.

How I build

AI-accelerated engineering, production-grade results.

I build the way the most productive engineers now build: I own the architecture, domain modeling, and system design, then direct modern AI coding tools to implement at speed — and I review, test, and harden everything myself. ReplocX wasn't conjured from a single prompt; it was decomposed into dozens of deliberate decisions — deterministic scoring, API versioning, data governance, CI/CD gates, and an end-to-end test suite. AI is the force multiplier. The judgment, the science, and the validation are mine. The payoff: research-grade methods shipped as real, inspectable products — faster than traditional workflows allow.

Flagship · full-stack

ReplocX — a research method, shipped as a product.

A deployed decision-support app that selects representative U.S. locations for national building-stock, climate, and heat-health simulation: deterministic scoring, representative-location allocation, ZIP-code explanation, weather-QC, scenario comparison, and exports — over a ~4,019-candidate national universe resolved to 20 strata, behind a versioned /api/v1 with CI gates and a public-demo / private-data split.

Live app ↗ Source ↗

From raw national data → an explainable decision

Every selected location is defended through scores, strata, alternatives, and substitution reasons. The same product surface serves a safe public demo and, behind auth, real analytical data.

Research case studies

Selected work from my peer-reviewed papers.

Indoor heat exposure, modeling methods, and the building-to-health link.

How hot do U.S. homes get — and who pays the health price?

Problem
People are exposed to heat indoors, yet residential overheating, comfort, and equity are poorly characterized.
Approach
Comprehensive review synthesizing U.S. evidence on overheating across thermal comfort, health, and socioeconomic vulnerability.
Impact
Reframes indoor heat as a measurable, equity-relevant exposure — the foundation for health-linkage-ready metrics.
Hampo, Schinasi & Hoque (2024). Surviving indoor heat stress in the United States. Heliyon 10(3).

Predicting indoor temperature & humidity: white-box vs. black-box

Problem
Stock-scale exposure estimates need models that are both accurate and tractable; physics and ML each have blind spots.
Approach
Critical review comparing white-box (simulation) and black-box (data-driven) prediction, with accuracy/data/interpretability trade-offs.
Impact
A methodological roadmap motivating the hybrid modeling at the core of my dissertation.
Hampo, Schinasi & Hoque (2025). Indoor temperature & humidity prediction in residential buildings. Building & Environment.

Overheating in U.S. residential buildings (simulation)

Problem
How does overheating vary across U.S. home types and climates — and where is heat-health risk concentrated?
Approach
EnergyPlus/OpenStudio building-performance simulation across typologies and climate zones.
Impact
Quantifies indoor overheating to target mitigation; presented at SimBuild 2026 (IBPSA-USA), Minneapolis.
Hampo, Schinasi & Hoque (2026). SimBuild 2026, IBPSA-USA Building Simulation Conference.

Do residential indoor temperatures affect health?

Problem
Evidence linking indoor temperature exposure to health is scattered across inconsistent observational studies.
Approach
Co-led scoping & systematic reviews structuring the indoor-temperature-and-health evidence base.
Impact
Defines the health endpoints my exposure metrics are designed to feed.
Edwards, De Roos, Hampo, et al. (2025). Science of the Total Environment 979.
